About Urdu Seekho

Urdu Seekho is a simple, text-only learning resource for people who speak Hindi and want to read Urdu confidently. It combines letter guidance with word-recognition practice.

How to start learning Urdu

Urdu and Hindi share much of their everyday speech. If you already speak Hindi, your main task is to recognise familiar words in the Urdu writing system. Urdu runs right to left, and letters often change their appearance when they join.

  1. Learn the letters: use the alphabet below to connect Urdu shapes with familiar Hindi sounds.
  2. Read real words: see Urdu words in their usual spelling, without added vowel marks.
  3. Check with Hindi: confirm how the word is read, and use English for meaning.
  4. Test yourself: practise 20 words at a time; revisit your mistakes.

Already read Qur'anic Arabic? Pay special attention to پ، چ، ٹ، ڈ، ڑ، گ، ں، ے، ھ and to Urdu text without zabar, zer or pesh.

How to read Urdu words: 5 simple rules

1. Start at the right

Urdu words and sentences are read from right to left. The word نام means नाम (name).

2. Joined letters can look different

A letter's written form may change depending on its position. Compare ب with با and اب. Focus on the dots and the overall shape.

3. Some letters break the connection

Letters such as ا، د، ر، و do not connect to the following letter. A gap inside a word does not always mean the word has ended.

4. Short vowels are usually not written

Ordinary Urdu normally omits the short vowel marks familiar to many Qur'an readers. For example, کتاب is किताब (book), not something you can always sound out confidently from the consonants alone. You learn the reading through familiar vocabulary and sentence context.

5. Look for Urdu-specific combinations

The character ھ helps form aspirated sounds: بھ = भ, کھ = ख. The character ں marks nasalisation, as in ماں = माँ.

Questions beginners often ask

Can Hindi speakers learn to read Urdu?

Yes. Everyday Hindi and Urdu share much vocabulary and grammar. The main early challenge is recognising the right-to-left script and words whose short vowels are not written.

Why does Urdu look different from Qur'anic Arabic?

Urdu uses a related script, often displayed in Nastaliq calligraphy, with additional letters for sounds such as پ، چ، گ. Ordinary Urdu also omits most short vowel marks, while many printed Qur'ans include them.

Do I need to know Arabic to read Urdu?

No. Arabic-script familiarity can help, but these lessons begin with the Urdu alphabet and Hindi sound equivalents.
